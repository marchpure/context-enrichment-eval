# Atlas Workspace Portfolio — TEST FIXTURE, not real commercial policy
Effective authority model, approved 2026-10-01. This fictional portfolio has eight independently contracted products. Never transfer a numeric promise, exception or price between products merely because the documents have the same outline.

## Which source wins
An approved, effective product-specific policy overrides a portfolio default only on explicitly named topics. An approved exception is limited to its named tenant, product, dates and scope. An archived policy is used only for events during its effective historical interval. A proposal without an approved effective date is not an operative rule. Meeting transcripts may record an approved decision, a rejection, an action item or an unresolved question; these states are different.

## Evidence and unknowns
Read the applicable product, plan, region, data class and incident date. Silence is not permission. Do not invent customer entitlements, reimbursement tariffs, data transfer approval, cryptographic certification or resolution guarantees. A support entitlement is not a document ACL. A pricing unit is not an invoice minimum. A retained audit event is not the retained document body.

## Scenario interpretation
All time durations are elapsed time unless the operative source explicitly specifies covered business minutes. When a request falls outside a business-hours support window, acknowledgement time starts at the next covered minute. The test scenarios explicitly exclude public holidays. A user's role, plan and workspace membership are separate inputs; evaluate all required guards before approving a data operation.

## Operational recording
Record the policy identifier, effective date, scope, decision and reason. A runbook helps execute a rule; it cannot silently widen the approved rule. Changing a draft into a promise needs a separate approval. If sources conflict without an explicit authority resolution, surface the conflict rather than averaging their numeric values.
